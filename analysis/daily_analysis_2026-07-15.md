<h3>Agentic AI is moving from pilot to core operating infrastructure</h3>
<p>Two of today's most significant stories show AI graduating from marketing gimmick to embedded operational decision-making. Very Group is rolling out agentic AI across its pricing strategy, promising faster, more transparent decisions at scale, while Walmart is using AI-powered digital twins to simulate and de-risk its global supply chain against conflict and weather disruption. Both signal that large retailers now trust autonomous AI agents with high-stakes, real-time commercial decisions rather than just back-office analytics.</p>
<div class='so-what'><span class='so-what-label'>So what for Value Retail</span>Agentic AI pricing and supply-chain simulation point toward a near-term capability for dynamic outlet pricing and inventory/replenishment forecasting across Villages — but also raise the bar for brand partners who will expect Bicester's own leasing and marketing systems to match this level of AI sophistication in rent negotiations, footfall forecasting and stock allocation.</div>

<h3>Generative AI is being reclaimed as a brand-voice and creative tool, not just automation</h3>
<p>Dollar Shave Club's Chief Brand and Innovation Officer is positioning generative AI as a way to protect and amplify brand personality in advertising, rather than homogenise it — a pushback against fears that AI creative tools flatten distinctive brand voice. This reflects a broader maturation in how brands think about AI: less about efficiency, more about creative reassertion.</p>
<div class='so-what'><span class='so-what-label'>So what for Value Retail</span>As brand partners increasingly use GenAI for campaign creative, Value Retail's own marketing and in-Village digital experiences (signage, personalised offers, concierge content) should ensure AI-generated content reinforces each brand's distinct identity rather than a generic "AI-outlet" aesthetic.</div>

<h3>Big retailers are curating elevated brand mixes through strategic third-party tie-ups</h3>
<p>Walmart's introduction of DTC darling Caraway cookware and Foot Locker's first-ever launch of technical outdoor brand Salomon both illustrate mainstream retailers deliberately upgrading assortment credibility by adding aspirational, premium-adjacent brands. This is a tenant-mix strategy playing out at scale — using selective, high-profile partnerships to shift brand perception upward.</p>
