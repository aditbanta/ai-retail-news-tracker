<h3>Physical retail expansion resumes even as tariff uncertainty clouds the outlook</h3>
<p>Tailored Brands' IPO filing and 500-store growth plan is a striking signal that mall and outlet-based retailers are betting on bricks-and-mortar again after years of closures. At the same time, Retail Dive's reporting on tariff-driven stockpiling and the "Q1 mirage" suggests this confidence is fragile: strong early-2025 comps were flattered by pre-tariff pricing and front-loaded inventory, with margin pressure and possible demand softness likely to surface later in the year. Together these stories point to a retail sector expanding its footprint while quietly hedging against cost shocks it knows are coming.</p>
<div class='so-what'><span class='so-what-label'>So what for Value Retail</span>Landlords should expect continued leasing appetite from apparel and tailoring brands looking for outlet formats to test expansion cheaply, but should stress-test tenant sales projections against tariff-driven price increases likely to bite in H2 2025 — full-price wholesale brands entering outlet may prove more resilient than pure-value plays.</div>

<h3>Third-party generative AI is quietly disintermediating brand-owned customer service</h3>
<p>Gartner's finding that consumer use of third-party GenAI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.) has doubled year-on-year, while proprietary brand chatbot usage has stagnated since 2022, is the most directly AI-relevant story in this set. It suggests shoppers increasingly trust general-purpose AI assistants over retailer-built tools for product research, comparison and even styling advice — a meaningful shift in who "owns" the customer relationship at the discovery stage.</p>
<div class='so-what'><span class='so-what-label'>So what for Value Retail</span>Brand partners' own apps and loyalty chatbots may lose influence over pre-visit discovery; Value Retail should ensure its villages, offers and stock information are structured (via schema, APIs, brand feeds) to be easily surfaced by third-party AI assistants, and consider its own AI concierge for itinerary planning, personal shopping and multilingual guest service to stay in the loop rather than be bypassed.</div>

<h3>Retailers are locking in long-term energy infrastructure to de-risk operations</h3>
<p>Walmart's first-ever nuclear power purchase agreement, aimed at securing stable, low-carbon energy for a distribution hub, reflects a broader retail trend of treating energy security as a strategic priority — partly driven by rising grid demand from data centres and AI compute, and partly by sustainability commitments and cost predictability.</p>
<div class='so-what'><span class='so-what-label'>So what for Value Retail</span>As villages invest further in digital infrastructure (in-store AI, personalisation, analytics), energy resilience and green power sourcing will become a differentiator worth discussing with brand partners and in ESG reporting, particularly for flagship villages with heavy footfall and growing tech deployment.</div>
